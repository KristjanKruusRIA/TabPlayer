# MIDI output to external ports (A26, THE-70)

Date: 2026-11-07. Branch `feature/a26-midi-out`, from develop `3700bfd`.

## What it does

- **Routing a track.** The Track inspector has a MIDI output section: choose a system MIDI port for the selected track, or keep the internal sound.
  - The choice is stored as `midiPort` on the track's mix, so it is one undoable edit, travels with the score, and choosing the current port records nothing.
  - A saved port that is not connected on this computer is shown as "(not connected)", and the track plays on the internal sound until the port is back. Refresh lists the ports again and moves tracks back onto a port that has returned.
- **What a routed track does.**
  - It is silent in the internal sound, with a 5 ms fade when the routing changes. Mute and solo apply to what is sent.
  - Its notes, program, volume (CC7, volume times the square root of the mix gain), pan (CC10, track and mix pan combined), sustain, bends and vibrato go to the port in time with playback.
  - Each pair of track and plan channel gets its own port channel, kept stable across changes. Percussion goes to channel 10, and several drum tracks on one port share it (the panel says so).
  - A track that does not fit in the 15 melodic channels falls back whole to the internal sound, and the panel says so.
- **Timing.**
  - The audio callback writes records into a heap-free ring. A sender thread writes each message at the moment its audio reaches the output: the earliest callback time in the last 1.5 s, plus one buffer, plus a user delay.
  - The delay is 0 to 500 ms in steps of 5, set in the panel, remembered for the app (not the score).
  - Count-in is covered.
- **Clean stops.** Stop, seek, loop wrap, pause, unmute, port loss, device switch and the end of a count-in all send note-offs, then sustain off, all sound off, all notes off and reset controllers, then re-state the channel and re-strike held notes where playback continues. Every message carries a generation, so nothing from before a reset is sent after it.
- **Failures.**
  - Ports are opened on their own threads, never under a lock.
  - A port that fails or does not open within 3 s is reported in the panel, and its tracks play internally.
- **Dependency.** `midir` 0.11.1 (MIT), listed in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

## Limits

1. **Latency model.**
   - A message is due at the earliest callback time in a 1.5 s window, plus one buffer, plus the user delay. Device and converter latency are not measured, so lining up with the speakers may need the delay.
   - The delay is global, lowered gradually, and not saved in the score.
   - Jitter depends on the sender waking on time: it sleeps, then spins the last 1.5 ms.
2. **After a stall or dropout.**
   - A shift of more than 50 ms is followed from the next callback.
   - A smaller shift is followed only after 5 callbacks in a row each more than a buffer behind, so up to 4 callbacks of messages go out early by up to 50 ms.
   - On a device with a buffer larger than about 50 ms plus one period, one callback that starts more than 50 ms late makes its own messages late by as much. That is corrected at the next callback and not counted as late.
3. **CPU.**
   - While the transport plays (count-in included) with something routed, the sender polls every 1 to 4 ms and spins up to 1.5 ms before each distinct message time, so dense bends cost more.
   - It also stays fast for 1 s after a port opens and after each mix change on a routed score.
   - It drops to 20 Hz one second after its last message. It stays fast if a stream stops calling back without reporting an error.
4. **Channels.**
   - Percussion shares channel 10, and about 7 to 15 melodic tracks fit per port.
   - Overlapping strikes of one key share one note-off.
   - A bend moves the whole channel.
5. **Re-stating and silencing.**
   - Seek, wrap, pause and resume, unmute, port recovery, device switch and the end of count-in re-strike held notes, which re-attack.
   - DIN ports manage about 1 ms per message.
   - Silencing (80 messages per port, at install, close and reset) runs synchronously, holds up other ports, and the late counters do not show that delay.
   - Silencing covers all 16 channels of the port, including channels other software uses.
6. **Return to the internal sound.** A track moved back from a port does not resume notes held at that moment.
7. **Not sent.** Drum-element mix, effects, master, auditions and the metronome are not sent. Exports never send MIDI, and the backing track cannot be routed. There is no MIDI input yet (U16, U17).
8. **Faults.**
   - Detection depends on the backend reporting a send error.
   - Retrying needs Refresh or a new prepare, not a fader move.
   - A hung driver open keeps its slot and blocks that port until it returns. It is reported after 3 s, and its thread is leaked.
   - A crash leaves notes sounding on the device.
9. **Port names.**
   - Ports are identified by name: the first of duplicate names is used.
   - WinMM truncates names to 31 characters.
   - Some drivers allow one open at a time.
   - There is a cap of 32 names per session.
   - Names with control or format characters cannot be routed.
10. **Untested.**
    - Real external hardware and synths.
    - The CoreMIDI and ALSA backends.
    - Large-buffer devices.
    - Real underruns: the stall logic is tested on synthetic timings.
    - Device loss.
    - A local Rust 1.88 build; CI covers it.

    No ear check against the internal sound was made.
11. **Compatibility.** Builds from before A26 refuse projects that contain `midiPort`.
