# GP8 parity A06: amp tone stack and measured amp and cabinet models (2026-10-24)

GP8's amp models have tone controls beside their gain. TabPlayer's amp had a model, drive and output gain only, and its four amp and three cabinet models had never been measured. This slice adds a four-band tone stack to the amp and measures every amp and cabinet model, in the native player and in the Sound task. It was implemented on branch `feat/gp8-parity`.

## Behavior
- **Tone stack:** the Amp block gains Bass, Mid, Treble and Presence, each from −12 to +12 dB in 0.5 dB steps. They shape the amp's output after its drive.
  - **Bass:** a low shelf at 100 Hz.
  - **Mid:** a broad peak at 650 Hz.
  - **Treble:** a high shelf at 2.5 kHz.
  - **Presence:** a one-octave peak at 4.5 kHz.
- **Flat by default:** every control starts at 0 dB, where the amp sounds exactly as before.
- **Edits:** each change is one undoable track-sound edit.
  - **While playing:** the tone glides to a new setting at 0.48 dB a millisecond at any sample rate. A full 24 dB swing takes 50 ms and does not ring the filters; a small change still arrives within the usual 10 ms.
  - **Back to flat:** once the filters have let go of the earlier tone, the amp's output goes back to the untouched path.
  - **Switching on:** switching the amp on starts its tone at its settings at once.
- **Saved documents and sounds:**
  - a control at 0 dB is not written, so sounds saved before this slice load, play and save unchanged;
  - saved sounds in My sounds, copied pedals and pasted sounds keep the tone;
  - the editor refuses the same values the native player does. Other blocks do not take tone fields.

## Evidence

  - **Tone curves:** each control at +12 dB on a clean amp at no drive, driven by a quiet sine, measured against a flat stack in dB:

    | Mid | 0.1 | 0.3 | 0.7 | 2.6 | 12.0 | 8.3 | 1.9 | 0.6 | 0.2 | 0.1 |

    The test checks each control at +12 and −12 dB: within 1 dB of the setting at its centre, and near 0 dB away from it.
  - **Amp models:** a 220 Hz sine at 0.1 peak, total harmonic distortion (harmonics 2–10) and output level:

    Each model's distortion rises with drive. Lead distorts more than crunch, and crunch more than clean, at every drive above 0. Crunch and lead hold their level within 1.5 dB from a quarter drive up.
  - **Cabinets:** response to sines against 1 kHz, in dB:

    | Cabinet | 40 Hz | 80 Hz | 200 Hz | 3 kHz | 5 kHz | 8 kHz |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | Open | −9.0 | −1.6 | 0.0 | −0.6 | −4.4 | −16.3 |
    | Closed | −14.2 | −4.1 | −0.2 | −1.9 | −9.8 | −25.4 |
    | Bass | −2.0 | −0.1 | 0.1 | −3.6 | −14.7 | −31.7 |

    The test requires the bass cabinet to keep the most low end and the open one the most top end, and every cabinet to roll off by more than 6 dB at 8 kHz.
  - **No clicks:** while a sine plays, the tone goes from flat to bass +12, then all four alternately at ±12, then the opposite, then flat. This runs through both `set_settings` and the prepared-configuration path. The largest step over the 62 ms after each change, the whole glide, is no more than 1.25× the larger settled step, and every sample is finite. Without the glide, the swing to the opposite setting fails this.
  - **Back to flat:** a 70 Hz sine plays while the tone returns from bass ±12, all four at +12, and alternating ±12 dB to flat, on both paths. Over the half second after each change, the output's largest second difference stays within 1.25× the settled one or 0.003, whichever is larger. Changing the filters at the start of the glide bends the output by up to about 0.0026, as the EQ's own ramp does. Before the fix, dropping what the filters still held when the stack turned flat bent it by 0.0126 to 0.0247, and the test fails.
  - **Switching on with a tone:** an amp switched on with bass +12 dB in the same edit that moves it from −12 dB matches, from 15 ms on and within 1e-4 of the peak, an amp switched on that was already set to +12 dB. Before the fix it glided in from −12 dB, at 0.125 against a 0.152 peak.
  - **Existing sounds:** the effect-order fingerprint of a chain with the amp on is unchanged, as are the DSP, modulation and tempo-sync tests.

## Review
- **First adversarial review:** changes requested, for one High, one Medium and several Low notes.
  - **High, fixed:** when the glide reached flat, the amp's output switched to the untouched path while the tone filters still held the end of the earlier boost or cut, which dropped it in one sample. On a 60 Hz sine with bass back from +12 dB, that was a step about 10 times the sine's own. The output now switches only once what the filters hold is below 1e-7, and a stack that was never used still takes the untouched path from the first frame.
  - **Medium, fixed:** the click test could not see this. It only watched the first 960 frames and used a 220 Hz sine. A new test watches the whole glide on a 70 Hz sine, and the first test now watches the whole glide too.
  - **Low, fixed:**
    - switching the amp on in the same edit as a tone change glided the tone in from its old settings; it now starts at its settings, with the tone filters at rest;
    - the glide's speed depended on the sample rate; it is now 0.48 dB a millisecond;
    - every tone filter was redesigned while any one glided; now only the ones that change are.
- **Second review:** approved.
  - **Checked:**
    - the switch to the untouched path cannot click;
    - it cannot flap, because flat filters at rest keep exactly zero state;
    - the glide is stable at 8, 48 and 192 kHz;
    - `process()` does not allocate.
  - **Low, kept:**
    - the start and end of a large glide bend the output slightly, by up to about 0.004 at 48 kHz, less than the EQ's own ramp bends;
    - two edits that both arrive before any audio frame can skip the switch-on reset, as they skip the amp's fade-in, because of how the previous and current settings are kept.

## Limits
- **Models:** still four amp and three cabinet models. GP8's amp topologies, power amp, cabinet impulse responses and microphone choices are not matched. The cabinets are fixed filters, not impulse responses.
- **One tone stack:** the same fixed tone curve for every model rather than each amp model's own circuit, and no interaction between controls as in a passive stack.
- **No listening comparison** with GP8's amps.
- **Older builds:** a sound with the tone stack in use cannot be opened by builds from before this slice.
