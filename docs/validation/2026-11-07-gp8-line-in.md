# GP8 parity: Line-In monitoring (A28, A29)

**Date:** 2026-11-07.
**Branch:** `feature/a28-line-in`, from `develop` at `b87c41b`.
**Linear:** THE-69, which also covered the tuner (U18, merged earlier).

## What changed

### Where it is

The Practice tools now have a **Line-In** section (`src/audio/LineInPanel.tsx`) below the tuner. A guitar or bass on an audio input is heard live through the app's output, through its own sound, while the score plays, is paused or is stopped. It offers:

- **Input:** the system default or any listed input. Changing it while monitoring moves monitoring to the new input.
- **Sound:** any factory preset, factory sound or saved sound from My sounds. The same amp and effects chain the tracks use processes the input.
- **Level:** 0 to 100%, default 80%.
- **Start / Stop monitoring**, with an input level meter and the measured latency while it runs.
- **Independence (A29):** the Line-In sound and level belong to the app, not to a score. They are remembered between sessions (`tabplayer.lineIn` in local storage) and never change when the track, the track's sound, the score or the document changes.
- **Lifetime:** monitoring always starts off. Once started it carries on while the Practice tools are closed and when another document is opened or created, so the player can play along. It ends on Stop and when the app closes. While it is on, the Practice button shows a dot and its name says "Line-In on".
- A note asks for headphones, because speakers near a microphone can feed back.

All text is in English, Estonian and German.

### Native path

- **Capture** (`src-tauri/src/audio_input.rs`, shared with the tuner): a cpal input stream on its own thread takes the loudest channel, as the tuner does, and decimates input above 48 kHz. Non-finite samples become silence before they enter the ring, and samples are held to ±4 when the ring is read.
- **Transport** (`src-tauri/src/audio/line_in.rs`):
  - A lock-free single-producer, single-consumer ring of atomic slots, sized 1 s.
  - A 4-point cubic resampler converts the input rate to the output rate. A controller on the ring fill nudges the ratio to hold about 15 ms of buffered input plus the largest recent output callback, and corrects drift between the two device clocks.
  - On underrun the sound fades out in 2 ms and fades back in after refilling. On overrun it fades, skips back to the target and fades in.
- **Sound and level:** the Line-In has its own `TrackDsp`, built off the audio thread from the chosen `TrackSound` and crossfaded in on a change. Level and the monitor switch glide over 5 ms. Tempo-synced effects follow the score's tempo, or 120 bpm without one.
- **Mixing:** the Line-In is added in the output callback after the score and any audition clip. A limiter on the Line-In alone (instant attack, 80 ms release) turns the guitar down only as far as keeps the sum within full scale. The score itself is never changed.
- **Independence:** the Line-In lives in the output transport, not in the score renderer, so it survives every score prepare, mix change and new document. A change of output device rebuilds it for the new rate.
- **No score yet:** starting Line-In before any score has prepared the output opens the output on its own, and the first score then plays into the same stream.
- **Commands:** `line_in_inputs`, `line_in_start(inputId)` (returns a session), `line_in_stop(session)`, `line_in_set({sound, level, monitor})`, `line_in_status()`. The session rules match the tuner's: a stale stop never ends a newer capture. Each settings change carries a ticket, and one with an older ticket never overwrites a newer one. Ticket order is not strictly the order of arrival over IPC, so the panel sends only one change at a time and keeps just the newest of quick changes.
- **Desktop test build only:** a synthesized "Test tone" input (`TABPLAYER_LINE_IN_TEST_TONE`).

## Evidence

- **Native tests** (`cargo test --lib`, 560 passed; `--test audio_line_in`, 12 passed):
  - **Realtime safety:** the output side makes zero heap operations across rate pairs 44.1→48, 48→44.1, 96→48 and 48→48. That covers a busy sound chain (amp, cabinet, chorus, delay, reverb), underrun, overflow, chain swaps, level changes, attach, replace, detach and retirement. Callbacks of 480 to 4096 frames go through the transport, and a headless `OutputCallback` is counted at 1536 and 4096 frames in mono, stereo and 6 channels, with a retirement partway through. The input side is zero for capture and decimation.
  - **Resampler:**
    - Pitch is within 1 cent across 44.1→48, 48→44.1, 32→48, 96→48 and 192→44.1.
    - A 30 kHz tone at 96 kHz is filtered rather than aliased.
    - Output callbacks of 480 to 4096 frames at 48→48, 44.1→48, 96→44.1, 192→44.1, 48→16 and 44.1→22.05, and sizes jittering between 256 and 4096, run without underruns or skips.
  - **Drift:** over 5 simulated minutes at ±0.5%, ±0.1% and 0% clock mismatch, the fill stays within 0.5 to 1.6 times the target after the first 10 s, with no underruns or skips.
  - **Clicks:** underrun fades to silence without a click and recovers; overflow skips back without a click.
  - **Score untouched:** with a silent monitor attached, a score with notes is bit-identical. With a quiet guitar, the output is score plus guitar within 2e-7. A loud guitar on a loud score stays within ±1.
  - **Independence:** a score install, a mix change and a loud master leave the Line-In level and sound unchanged. A device rebuild keeps sound, level and capture at the new rate. An attach or detach that lands while the output reopens reaches the new stream.
  - **Attach partway through a callback:** a monitor attached after the first block of a 1536- or 4096-frame callback stays silent for the rest of it, then runs 40 s in step with no underruns or skips.
  - **Faults:** huge or non-finite input is clamped. A chain that goes non-finite falls silent on the audio thread, is counted in `resets`, and is replaced by a fresh chain from the control side, which fades in from silence. Changes made while the output stream is failed or backed up (8 messages queued) are recorded, not queued, and the newest is sent once the stream drains.
  - **Commands:** settings are validated, and repeated or stale changes are harmless. The session and stop rules hold, including a hung start, a stop during a start and a capture that ends by itself. An end-to-end tone goes from the capture thread through the transport to the output at the right pitch and level.
  - **Tuner:** after the capture refactor, the tuner tests and its desktop driver still pass.
- **Frontend tests** (`src/audio/LineInPanel.test.tsx`, 15 passed):
  - Preferences read back, with damaged data falling back to defaults.
  - Quick changes: only the newest is sent, never an older one after a newer one.
  - A monitor still running natively is picked up after a reload and can be stopped.
  - A stop overtaking a start ends that start, with no error shown when the native side then refuses it.
  - A Stop pressed while moving to another input is still closing the old capture is kept.
  - A newer change queued behind one that fails is still sent.
  - The sound and level are sent before monitoring starts.
  - The level meter and latency are shown.
  - Changing the input moves monitoring to the new one.
  - Changes during a pending start are kept.
  - A remembered sound is kept when its source is gone.
  - Failed starts, refused sounds and stopped devices are reported.
  - Unmounting the panel, as a document switch does, keeps monitoring, and the Practice button's indicator follows it.
- **Desktop** (`tests/desktop/parity-line-in.mjs`, Windows desktop-test build; the test input is a 220 Hz sine at 0.35 amplitude, at 48 kHz):
  - The output device reported 192 kHz, so the run included a 48→192 kHz conversion.
  - With the clean sound at 80%, the Line-In reached the output callback at about −10.8 dBFS, with about 35 ms reported latency.
  - At 20% it fell to −23.1 dBFS, and it stayed at −23.1 dBFS while the score played.
  - It kept running with the Practice tools closed and after a New document, with the Practice button's indicator on. Across play, close and the new document there were 0 underruns, skips, dropped samples and resets. It stopped on Stop and the indicator cleared.

The audio integration tests (`audio_line_in`, `audition`, `master`, `transport`, `render`, `dsp`) pass. `npm run typecheck` passes. The panel, tuner, editor panel and i18n Vitest files pass. ## Review

The audio-thread path, the capture thread and the session commands are realtime and untrusted-input code, so a fresh read-only reviewer checked them adversarially, three rounds in all.

- **Round 1:** not approved. Findings:
  - **High:** large output callbacks could underrun.
  - **High:** a document switch ended monitoring.
  - **High:** changing the input did not restart capture.
  - Five Mediums and some Lows.

  All were fixed.
- **Round 2:** not approved. Findings:
  - **High:** a monitor attached partway through a callback was out of step.
  - **Medium:** changes queued without limit to a stream that was failed or not draining.
  - **Low:** latency was reported half a callback low.
  - **Low:** the audio thread reset a broken chain itself.
  - Frontend Lows.

  All were fixed.
- **Round 3: approved.** Three Lows remained, all fixed afterwards:
  - Stop during a pending start showed a false "could not start" message.
  - A Stop pressed while a restart was closing the old capture could be overridden.
  - A fresh chain started without a fade-in.

  Each now has a test.

## Limits

1. **Latency is an estimate.** It counts the input buffered at the start of the callback, the resampler, the decimation filter and one output buffer. It leaves out the input device's own buffer and any output or driver latency beyond one buffer. No loopback measurement was made; the 35 ms is that estimate.
2. **Latency grows with the device buffer.** It is at least the 15 ms target (up to 40 ms with large input blocks) plus the largest recent output callback; 4096 frames at 48 kHz add about 85 ms. Callbacks longer than about 0.3 s cannot be buffered and will underrun.
3. **Attaching partway through a callback:** a monitor attached then is silent for the rest of that callback.
4. **The score is never turned down.**
   - On a loud score, a limiter with no lookahead (instant attack, 80 ms release) turns the guitar down to whatever headroom is left. With the master ceiling at −1 dBFS that can be as little as about 0.11 of full scale.
   - The guitar can therefore pump with the score's peaks and is slightly shaped at each overshoot.
   - The output level shown is measured before this limiter.
5. **Monitoring only.** The Line-In bypasses the master bus and is not recorded, exported or rendered.
6. **Input:**
   - It is mono, taken from the loudest channel with 6 dB hysteresis.
   - There is no channel choice and no input trim.
   - The level applies after the sound chain, so how hard the amp and drive are hit depends on the interface gain.
7. **Sound and input choices:**
   - Only the effects of the chosen sound are used; its voice and drum kit mean nothing here.
   - Editing a saved sound doesn't reach a running Line-In until the sound is chosen again.
   - A deleted sound keeps playing as "Last used sound".
   - A saved input that is missing is shown as unavailable, and Start fails until another input is chosen.
8. **Faults and repair:**
   - The Line-In is silent while the output is faulted. It resumes with the latest recorded settings when a device is selected or a score is prepared.
   - On a stream that is alive but not draining, changes wait until fewer than 8 messages are queued.
   - Deferred changes, and the replacement of a chain that went non-finite, happen at the panel's 200 ms status poll. That poll slows if the webview is throttled. Until then a broken chain is silent, and its replacement carries no state over.
   - Known gap: a compressor driven to about 3e38 without producing a non-finite value would go silent without a reset. The ±4 limit at the ring read prevents this input from reaching it.
9. **Memory:** each chain holds a 16 s delay line, about 6 MB at 48 kHz and 25 MB at 192 kHz. A sound change briefly holds several chains, and up to 8 can be queued while a stream stalls.
10. **Meter readings** are peaks since the previous status read, shared by every reader. So a single read can show −120 dBFS without a dropout. The desktop driver therefore requires audible readings and checks the underrun counters separately.
11. **Before any score has been prepared,** starting the Line-In opens the output on its own.
12. **No feedback protection** beyond the note asking for headphones.
13. **Evidence:**
    - The evidence comes from one Windows machine, one 192 kHz output and a synthetic 48 kHz tone. There was no real interface or guitar, and no macOS or Linux run.
    - The most exposed setups are covered only by headless tests: about equal input and output rates with callbacks of 1024 frames or more, and attaching partway through a callback.
    - Clock drift is simulated; there is no long run against two real clocks.
    - The Line-In was not judged by ear.
