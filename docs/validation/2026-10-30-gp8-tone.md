# GP8 parity: driven amps, pedals and cabinets rebuilt (2026-10-30)

The user judged the clean tones good but the drive unusable next to GP8. This slice replaces the driven half of the chain. It follows the design in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-24-gp8-tone-design.md`. Claude implemented it with an agent, had it reviewed, and ran it on a Windows desktop build. The user compared the A/B renders by ear and said the new ones are much better. That listening is the acceptance: the measurements only show the design landed where it aimed.

## Behavior
- **Amps:** `crunch` and `lead` improve in place, and `metal` and `bassDrive` are new. `clean` and `bass` stay on the original path.
- **Pedals:** the overdrive, distortion and fuzz names stay and their circuits change.
- **Cabinets:** `closed` improves in place, and `combo` and `bassStack` are new. `open` and `bass` keep their filters.
- **Sources:** everything is synthesized. No impulse response, captured data or dependency was added.
- **Factory sounds:** `driven-bass` is new, and seven driven sounds are retuned.
- **Changing a sound while it plays:** two running chains crossfade, and the audio thread allocates nothing and takes no lock.
- **Saved documents:** a document using an older model name loads and takes the better sound.

## Evidence
- **Rust:** 841 tests passed on the merged source, none failed.
- **Frontend:** `tsc` was clean. `src/App.test.tsx` and `src/audio` passed 949 tests with 1 skipped, at a 60-second timeout.
- **Clean tones:** the clean renders were compared with the old build's renders during the slice.
- **Desktop:** these drivers passed on a Windows desktop-test build of the merged source:
  - `tests/desktop/parity-amp-tone.mjs`;
  - `tests/desktop/parity-drive-effect.mjs`, where overdrive, distortion and fuzz each raised sustain and flattened crest against the dry signal;
  - `tests/desktop/parity-factory-sounds.mjs`, where metal rhythm sat within 6 dB of jazz clean and carried far more upper-octave power.

## Review
- **First hot-path review:** rejected the audio-thread changes.
- **Findings and fixes:**
  - **High, fixed:** a swap arriving during a crossfade clicked.
  - **Medium, fixed:** a silent track still ran the whole chain.
  - **Medium, fixed:** a test was too loose to catch the swap failure.
- **Re-review:** approved, with one Medium on CPU cost and some Lows, listed under Limits.
- **Desktop run:** the factory sounds driver first failed on `lead` against `metal`. It had launched an older binary from the harness path, so all three drivers were rerun on the merged executable. This was a test setup error, not a tone bug.

## Limits
- **Why this stays a judgment call:** GP8's amp models are not reproduced. The target is the user's ear, with installed plug-ins and an IR pack used only to check broad features.
- **CPU:** 8 panned high-gain tracks cost about 54% of a core, and 16 about 107%. A CPU 1.85 times slower would overrun on 8. Overload means crackle only, and no load readout exists yet, because cpal reports no underruns on WASAPI.
- **Cabinets:** only the built-in synthesized cabinets exist. User-loaded impulse responses are a separate slice.
- **Review Lows, open:**
  - the parked-mix clause has no test with a replace after the ramp ends;
  - the `.max(apart)` term and the 0.035 constant in `audio_tone.rs` are not scaled for every rate;
  - paused-ramp parking is undocumented;
  - `Biquad` has no flush;
  - there is no full-queue test, and no rest or chained-swap tests at 192 kHz.
