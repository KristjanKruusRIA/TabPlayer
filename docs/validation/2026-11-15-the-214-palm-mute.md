# THE-214: palm mutes and dead notes sound like string noise

Date: 2026-11-15. Branch `agy/the-214-palm-mute`. These are measurements. Nobody has listened to the result yet; the owner's ears decide whether it is fixed.

## Cause

The owner plays a distorted rhythm guitar tuned drop D one octave low (strings D1 to E3; the palm mutes lie between MIDI 26 and 47). Of 400 palm-muted notes in the owner's score, 370 are below E2 (MIDI 40), the lowest recorded root. Three things combined:

1. `plan.ts` gives a palm mute the alphaTab length of about 83 ms. At note end the renderer started the string-release recording as an ordinary, unfiltered note. The mute's own sound is only a few tens of milliseconds long, so the release recording was the loudest and longest thing heard: through the metal-rhythm sound it was 11 dB louder than the mute's own tail, and below E2 it was pitched down (up to 2.25 times longer) and rang for more than a second.
2. Every recording opens with about 20 ms of near-silence before the pick. Pitched down to D1 that silence becomes about 45 ms, longer than a dead note (31 ms) and about half of a palm mute. The decay envelope and the note end were timed from the start of the recording, so the pick arrived after the note had ended and was faded away. A dead note below E2 peaked at -41 to -62 dBFS without the release recording; what was heard was the release noise.
3. The mute was cut at its written end instead of ringing like a damped string, and its tone (1 kHz low-pass, decay 0.10 s) was neither as dark nor as loud as the reference.

## Changes

`src-tauri/src/audio/renderer.rs`:

- No release recording is selected for `Muted` and `Dead` notes (selection at construction, so nothing changes on the audio thread).
- Starting a note on a string cuts any palm mute or dead note still sounding on the same string of the same track (`SampleVoice::cut_ring`), with a 4 ms fade, whatever the recording's own release is. That includes one whose own note ends in the same frame: alphaTab ends a palm mute at 83 ms or at the beat's end, whichever is first, so in a fast run (16th triplets at 120 BPM, 16ths at 180, 32nds at 90) a mute ends exactly where the next note starts, and before this the old mute then began its 0.2 s ring under the new note. Nothing else is cut.
- The lead-in of a muted or dead note's recording and the voice's trims are measured once while the renderer is prepared, once per recording (a cache keyed by the recording), then handed to `SampleVoice::prepared`. Starting or restarting a note (after a seek) scans nothing.

`src-tauri/src/audio/voices.rs` (`SampleVoice`; muted and dead only, every other articulation takes the same code path as before):

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Timing of the recording | played from its start | played from its start, exactly as every other note (the recorded lead-in is kept) |
| Decay, ring and cut | counted from the start | counted from the pick (the start plus the lead-in at the sounding pitch: 20 ms at E2, 45 ms at D1) |
| Muted ring | none (release at 83 ms) | rings until 0.2 s after the pick, then the region's own release |
| Dead note | released at 31 ms | held until 30 ms after the pick, then the region's own release |
| Muted decay | 0.10 s | 0.30 s |
| Muted low-pass | 1 kHz, fixed | 4 harmonics above the note, limited to 140 Hz to 1 kHz (D1 at 150 Hz, E2 at 330 Hz, E3 at 660 Hz, A3 and up 1 kHz) |
| Muted gain (black guitar) | 0.8 | 1.0 |
| Dead decay | 0.025 s | 0.05 s (low-pass 650 Hz unchanged) |
| Dead gain (black guitar) | 0.55 | 1.6 from the recording's root up, falling in dB (0.6 dB per semitone) to 0.6 at 14 semitones below it |
| A join (slide, hammer-on) between a muted or dead note and another technique, or between two dead notes across the root | the first note's gain and tone | the sounding technique's gain, trim, low-pass and decay, gliding over about 4 ms |

Timing rule: every note, whatever its technique, plays its recording from the start, so the pick lands a recording's lead-in after the written start for all of them. Palm mutes, dead notes, open notes, the Light (staccato) setting and every other voice therefore stay in time with each other, exactly as on `develop`: switching Normal and Light moves no note. A test pins that the pick of a muted, a dead and an open note at one tick agree within 1 ms at E2 and at D1 (20.0 and 44.9 ms after the start, which is what `develop` did for the muted note). Removing the lead-in from every note, so that notes sound at their written start, would be a separate change for the owner to decide; it was not done here and the lead-in remains a timing error of up to 45 ms on a low string for every technique.

Per voice (`technique_trims_db` in `voices.rs`, applied by the articulation a voice sounds now, not in `tone.rs`). The black guitar (`black-guitar`, General MIDI programs 26 to 31) serves the clean, crunch and distorted electric programs alike; only the effect chain differs. So its balance is the one that is right dry and clean (palm mute a little under an open note, dead note under it), and what that costs through metal-rhythm is small, because that chain saturates the level anyway (+1.3 dB of palm mute over open at gain 1.0 against +1.4 dB at 1.3). The other voices sit lower so a palm mute stays about as far from an open note as before and a dead note clearly under it:

### Interpretation "Palm mute" (Normal, Light, Heavy)

- Normal: palm mute (`muted`), as above.
- Light: becomes `staccato` in `interpretation.ts`: bright (7 kHz), short, with its release recording and its lead-in. Unchanged. It is now about 3 times brighter than Normal in the band-energy test and Normal rings longer; a Rust test pins that.
- Heavy: the palm mute at 0.8 of the velocity, about 2.3 dB softer than Normal. Unchanged logic; a Rust test pins that it differs from Normal by more than 1.5 dB.

## Measurements

Method. A scratch harness (not in the repository) renders the owner's score, track one (metal-rhythm factory sound), through `Renderer`, each onset analysed over a window of `min(gap to the next onset, 250 ms)`. So every figure is an average over that window, not over the note: a palm mute of 83 ms with a gap of 250 ms is averaged over 250 ms, an open note over at most the same, and RMS over a short sound is lower than its peak by several dB. Only notes at MIDI 47 or below with a gap of at least 110 ms are counted (200 palm mutes, 229 open notes). The reference is the owner's Guitar Pro export of the same bars, aligned by onset search (+91 ms). "Before" is `develop`. "+100 ms" is the level 100 ms after the strike against the loudest 5 ms of the window; "dec +100" is the level 100 ms after the envelope's own peak. Band shares are shares of the spectrum of the window. For the synthetic isolated notes (1.5 s apart, median by key band MIDI 26-39 / 40-47 / 48 up) the same window is 250 ms, and peak figures use 150 ms.

## Tests

`src-tauri/tests/audio_palm_mute.rs` (19 tests), `audio_palm_mute_identity.rs` (1) and a unit test in `src/audio/voices.rs`, on synthetic recordings with the recorded lead-in and a loud release recording, needing no fetched audio (shared fixtures in `tests/palm_common/mod.rs`):

- no release voice for muted and dead notes (render with and without the release recording is bit-identical; an open note still releases);
- level at +100 ms between -17 and -3 dB below the loudest frame and gone (below -50 dB) by 400 ms, keys 26, 33, 40, 47;
- the pick of a muted, a dead and an open note at one tick lands within 1 ms of each other and of the 20 / 44.9 ms `develop` had, at E2 and D1;
- palm mute darker than open (bright-to-low energy ratio under half) and between -8.5 and -4.0 dB against the open note, dry, keys 26 to 52 (measured -5.1 dB at D1 falling to -7.3 dB at E3 on this synthetic pair; the old gain 0.8, 1 kHz low-pass and cut at 83 ms gave -13.5 dB at D1);
- dead note within 14 dB of the open note's peak at keys 26 to 39 and also 40, 52, and gone by 150 ms; dead peak at least 3 dB under the open note's at keys 26 to 52, dry and through a compressor in front of a clean amp (the factory funk-clean chain without its wah, on which a fixed dead gain of 2.0 puts a D1 dead note 2.8 dB under the open note and fails it); a six-string dead chord's peak at least 3 dB under an open chord's and both under 0.9 (so the master limiter is not engaged), for standard and the owner's tuning;
- the dead gain neighbouring keys differ by under 1 dB across the root for roots E2, B1 and E1, falling 8.5 dB over 14 semitones and no further;
- back-to-back palm mutes (16th triplets at 120 BPM, 16ths at 180, 32nds at 90 and 16ths at 200, i.e. 83.3 and 75 ms, no gap) on the guitar and the bass at D1 and E2: the first mute's own sound is more than 24 dB down from 10 ms after the next note starts; with the old rule (cut only a ring that has begun) it was still at -3.5 dB;
- a same-string re-strike cuts the ring by 0.24 s on all four voices, the bass recordings given their real 0.25 s release (the old test measured at 0.30 to 0.33 s and could not fail); with the cut fading over the recording's release instead of 4 ms it fails;
- trims follow the sounding articulation through joins in both directions, bit-identical once the glide has ended; the low-pass, the gain and the decay glide over a few ms into and out of a palm mute or dead note (and between two dead notes across the root), and a join between two open techniques is not smoothed;
- no lead-in is measured while starting notes, seeking and playing (`lead_in_scans`, a thread-local counter in `voices.rs`);
- per voice trims exact to 0.05 dB, open notes identical across voices;
- Light, Normal and Heavy stay distinct; no heap use while palm mutes and dead notes ring.

Bit-identity, in two parts. Portable: a unit test in `src/audio/voices.rs` (`legacy_equivalence`) keeps a verbatim copy of `develop`'s voice and runs it next to the new one for every technique but the palm mute and the dead note (ten articulations, four keys, 44.1 and 48 kHz), with a bend, a join into another technique, a seek, a release and a loop, comparing every output frame and the source position bit for bit in the same process, so it holds on any platform's maths library. Whole renderer, Windows only (`audio_palm_mute_identity.rs`): a four-second phrase of two tracks (black guitar and darkblack bass) with open, legato, staccato, ghost, tapped, harmonic and feedback notes, a bend, a 1.6 s ringing note and a seek into held notes, rendered at 44.1 and 48 kHz, fingerprinted on `develop` (`0xf7591afeab7eb66b` and `0x6dedeeca1d4b2167`) and equal after the change; elsewhere that test only checks that the phrase sounds, because the renderer's `sin`, `exp` and `powf` come from the platform's maths library and a platform that rounds differently would show a different hash with no change in behaviour (the drum-mix baseline has the same property). Mixed phrases (with palm mutes) may differ in the last bits from `develop` wherever a muted voice sums with an open one, because the muted voice is a different signal; both tests contain none. The fingerprints were captured on Windows only.

When the first nine tests of the earlier version were run on the unchanged `develop` code, seven failed. The pick-timing, dead-chord and per-voice tests were written first against the previous version of this change and failed there (muted pick at 1.08 ms instead of 20 ms; dead chord not under the open chord); the thresholds of the level tests were set after measuring the new behaviour on the synthetic voice, not before.

## Remaining gap, and what could not be matched

- The metal-rhythm chain flattens the level: the chain output is saturated, so palm mute against open moves from -0.1 dB to +1.3 dB, and by under 1.5 dB over the whole sweep of voice gain 0.8 to 3.0 (11.5 dB at the input). The reference has +9.9 dB. Voice-level changes do not close this; it is a question for the owner about `tone.rs` (gain staging of the metal amp, the high-pass in front of it). I did not push the voice to extreme settings, because that would damage the dry and clean sounds.
- Through metal-rhythm the mute is about 1.8 times darker than open (centroid 599 Hz against 1056 Hz) where the reference is about 4.5 times (117 against 521 Hz), and 15.5% of its energy is still at 1 to 4 kHz against 0.1%. That content is made by the distortion from the low string, not by the voice. Its level +100 ms after the strike is -4.8 dB against the reference's -3.7 dB.
- Sub-80 Hz content through the amps: the amp chain removes it (a `tone.rs` property). The reference keeps 14% of the mute below 80 Hz; ours has 0.2% through metal-rhythm. Not changed.
- The overall rhythm tone is about 10 dB quieter and brighter than the reference (open notes: -32 dB against -24 dB RMS, centroid 1056 Hz against 521 Hz). Not changed.
- The sample range starts at E2. Everything below it is the E2 recording pitched down; the owner's palm mutes are D1 to D#2. No samples were added.
- The recorded lead-in remains a timing error for every note (up to 45 ms after the written start at D1); it is the same for all techniques now. Removing it is the owner's decision.
- Dry level at +100 ms is lower than the reference's amplified figure; the staccato recording's own decay limits it (decay 0.3 against 0.1 changes it by about 2 dB).
- One trim per voice, not per program: the black guitar serves clean and distorted electric alike, so a distorted preset on it gets the dry and clean balance. The trims were set from the synthetic notes and one score. A bass dead note through a guitar clean chain peaks above its open note (chain property, see above); the dry bass dead note is quiet (-18 to -26 dB over 250 ms).
- The 1-4 kHz band of a dry or clean low palm mute is 20 dB under the reference's amplified one and 8 dB under `develop`'s (see above); not changed.
- The table comes from one owner score; a second owner score, a normal-tuned riff and a multi-track riff were rendered before and after as clips only, which nobody has listened to. The harness, the owner's scores and every render stay out of the repository.
